Regulation RAG Checker

Ask a question about the GDPR or the EU AI Act. The system finds the article that answers it, writes a short cited answer, and refuses when the texts don't cover the question. This page is the evidence: every experiment, every failure, every number.

01

How it works

Six stages between a question and an answer. The two amber stages are safety gates: one cheap check before the language model is called, and one check on what it writes.

02

Ten experiments, one at a time

Each change was tested on the same 44 development questions with a known answer. Bars show how often the right article was found. The chips say what each change did, the small number is the change against the run it was compared with, and the starred row is what the final system uses.

One question is worth about 2 points on this set, so differences of one question are noise. The largest gains came from searching the articles only, a stronger embedding model, and rewriting the question in legal vocabulary. A re-ranker made results worse and was rejected.

03

From finding to answering

The answer step writes a short reply from the top five passages and must cite them. Refusing when the right article never reached the model is the correct behaviour, so refusals are split by whether the right article was among the passages.

04

The "I don't know" cutoff

If the best search score is below the cutoff, the system refuses without calling the language model. No single cutoff separates real questions from off-topic ones, so the model's own judgement is a second gate.

Attacking my own system
